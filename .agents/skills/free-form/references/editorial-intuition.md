# Editorial intuition

How to decide what a line needs. The engine will render whatever you ask for; these are the questions
that stop it becoming decoration.

## 1. Find the job of each line

Read the cleaned transcript sentence by sentence and name what each one does for the viewer.

| Job | How to recognise it | What the viewer needs | First treatment to consider |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hook | The first seconds; the claim that earns the watch | A reason to stay, with the face on screen | `punch` on the one surprising word |
| Question | "What is...", "Why does..." | A beat to hold the question | `card` with a `?` glyph |
| Definition | "X means...", "X stands for..." | To see the term taken apart | `expand` when the parts are spoken; else `card` |
| Claim | The sentence the video exists to say | Weight and stillness | `card`, or `punch` if one word carries it |
| Contrast | "A does this, B does that", "but", "versus" | Both sides visible at once | `versus` |
| Sequence | "First... then...", steps, a list of three | Order and progress | `list` beside the speaker |
| Example | "For example...", a story | The speaker; examples are personal | Stacks; a `list` or `versus` only if it has structure |
| Turn | "But in all honesty...", "the truth is" | A change of temperature | Change of camera framing; a `whip`; an accent `card` |
| Payoff | The answer to the hook | To recognise the callback | Repeat the hook's word in a new form |
| Connective | "This is the software that..." | Nothing extra | Plain stacks, at most one `mark` |

Most sentences are connective. That is correct: treatments stand out because the lines around them
are plain.

## 2. Carry or connect

Mark each line **carry** (remove it and the video loses its point) or **connect**. Only carry lines
get a scene. A typical 60 s explainer has 4 to 7 carry lines.

## 3. Fit the treatment to the shape of the content

- Use a structured scene only when the speech has that structure. Two things compared: `versus`. Parts
  named one by one: `expand`. Ordered steps: `list`. Do not invent a third list item or a second side
  the speaker never said.
- Text on a takeover that is not the speaker's exact words (list items, side labels) must be a faithful
  shortening of them. Four words or fewer per item.
- Takeovers hide the face. Use them when the idea is more interesting than the expression; stay on the
  speaker for emotion, humour, a gesture that explains, and the first second of the video.
- The hero word is the word a viewer would repeat to a friend, not the longest or the loudest.

## 4. Budget

| Thing | Guide for 60 s | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Takeovers | 4 to 6, at most 35% of runtime | The speaker is the video |
| Same scene type twice in a row | Never | It reads as a template |
| Same transition twice in a row | Avoid | Variety is the point; a callback is the exception |
| `punch` | 1 to 3 | Each one spends attention |
| `mark` | About 1 per 12 s | More and none of them is emphasis |
| Hero words in stacks | About 1 per 10 s | As above |
| Longest stretch with no change at all | 4 s | Shots, stacks or a scene must move |
| Shortest takeover | 1.2 s | Shorter cannot be read |

Educational speech needs room. When a line is dense, slow the edit down around it.

## 5. Transitions carry meaning

| Transition | Use it for |
| --- | --- |
| `cut` | The default. Continuing the same thought |
| `flash` | A sharp interruption: a question, a correction |
| `push` (in, and out) | Opening a new section; the scene has several steps |
| `zoom` | Landing on a conclusion or the repeated claim |
| `wipe` | Revealing an answer or an idea that widens the frame |
| camera `whip` | A change of topic while staying on the speaker |
| camera `jump` | A new sentence in the same topic |

Pair them with the content's rhythm: a build ends in a `zoom` or `punch`; a new section starts with a
`push`. Never pick a transition because it has not been used yet.

## 6. Make it this video

- **Colour.** Take the accent from the footage (clothing, a light, a prop) or the brand, and check it on
  the paper and ink backgrounds. One accent only.
- **Framing.** Find the face on a still and set `camera.faceY`. If hands gesture in the lower third,
  prefer top slots for stacks during those lines.
- **Glyphs.** Choose a glyph that says something about the line (`?`, `!`, `½`, `+`, `=`, `→`, a
  number). No glyph is better than a generic one.
- **Callbacks.** If the hook returns in the body, give it the same word in a different treatment so the
  viewer feels the return.
- **Sound.** One cue per scene entrance and per revealed item, none on ordinary caption words. Sound
  follows picture events; never add a cue with nothing to see.

## 7. Critique before rendering

Look at every contact sheet and answer honestly:

1. Cover the captions with your hand. Does each takeover still tell you what the line is about?
2. Is any treatment used because it was available rather than because the line needed it? Remove it.
3. Is there a carry line that got nothing? Is there a connective that got a scene?
4. Any text over the eyes or mouth, off the frame, or smaller than its neighbours for no reason?
5. Any screen that sits nearly empty for more than a second? Start the scene later.
6. Read only the hero words and takeover text in order. Do they summarise the video?
7. Would a viewer with the sound off follow the argument?

Fix what fails, then render. One good draft per round of feedback.

## 8. Say what the edit still lacks

After the draft, tell the user plainly what would lift it further and needs them: B-roll or a screen
recording at a named moment, a licensed music track, a retake of a line, a thumbnail. Do not fake any
of these.
